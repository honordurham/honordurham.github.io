Hi! My name is Honor Durham, and I am a Senior Research Support Associate (RSA) at the [MIT Election Data + Science Lab](https://electionlab.mit.edu/). I graduated from the University of Wisconsin-Madison in May 2025 with degrees in political science and data science. My primary research interests lie in American politics with a focus on elections. I am fascinated by American elections (election administration and voter behavior), place-based partisanship and the impacts of local community structures on political outcomes in both the United States and United Kingdom, and democratic backsliding in modern democracies. Outside of political science, I enjoy podcasting, statistics and R/Python. I plan on applying to graduate school (Political Science PhD!) during the 2026-2027 academic year with the goal of starting Fall 2027. When I have some free time, I like to spend my time [reading](https://www.goodreads.com/user/show/140832370), running, [crocheting](../images/crochet.JPG), and/or spending time with loved ones.

As an RSA at MEDSL, I am responsible for various data collection and cleaning tasks, provide research assistance, and assist with project management. Some of the lab projects that I have worked on include the [Elections Performance Index](https://elections.mit.edu/#/data/map), the [2024 Precinct Project](https://github.com/MEDSL/2024-elections-official), and the [2026 Stanford-MIT Elections Performance Central](https://electionlab.mit.edu/research/projects/elections-performance-central). I have also been a part of MEDSL's "Propositions Project," which has involved collecting and extracting data from state voter guides for both on and off-cycle elections with a primary focus on more recent elections. I am also individually working on a project on local woman candidate vote shares across different office types. I presented this project at the [2026 Election Science, Research, and Administration (ESRA) Conference](https://esra-conference.org) and was the runner up for Best Poster. 

Being a part of the [Department of Political Science at MIT](https://polisci.mit.edu/) has been a life changing experience. I have had the opportunity to attend department talks, audit graduate-level classes (Quant 1 and Comparative Field Seminar), and learn more about what it is like to do political science research full time. It has only furthered my determination to pursue a PhD in political science and to be a part of the political science academic community.

Please feel free to reach out to me at **hdurham@mit.edu** if you want to connect!
